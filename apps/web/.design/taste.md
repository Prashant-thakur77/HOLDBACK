# HOLDBACK web: taste profile

The filter for every design decision. If a choice fails this page it is changed or dropped,
however good it looks elsewhere.

## Personality

The site IS:

- **Unhurried.** The product's gift is time, so the page never rushes anyone. Large quiet
  fields of tinted paper, one thing happening at a time, a window that drains at a steady rate
  you can watch without anxiety.
- **Accountable.** Every claim carries its receipt. Rule lines, serial numbers, dates, sources
  and hashes are visible parts of the layout, set like a ledger, not hidden in a footer.
- **Neighbourly.** It speaks like the helpful person at the counter, in your language. Everyday
  ticket and passbook colours, ink that is a little uneven, plain words, numerals in your script.

The site must NEVER feel:

- **Alarmist.** No red full-screen warnings, sirens, skulls, hoodies or flashing countdowns.
  Urgency is how scams work; we do the opposite.
- **Crypto-hype.** No neon on black, glowing orbs, 3D coins, purple-to-teal washes or
  "futuristic" HUD chrome.
- **Bureaucratic.** The form grammar must never turn into a government portal: no cold grey, no
  walls of small print, no twelve-field forms.

## Audience taste

- **Judges** live in explorers, docs and terminals. They trust what they can verify: a program
  id they can paste, a hash they can recompute, a number with its script. They have seen a
  hundred dark gradient landing pages this month; a light, printed-looking page with real data
  stands out and reads as confidence.
- **Users in India** pay by scanning a code at a shop and hear the payment said aloud by the
  speaker on the counter. They know the passbook, the bus ticket, the post office counter. Big
  numerals, flat bright colour and Hindi that is set properly read as trustworthy; tiny grey
  English does not.
- **Users in Latin America** know instant bank transfers, the payment slip that takes days to
  clear, the lottery ticket, the crossed cheque. Spanish and Portuguese strings run longer than
  English, so nothing is designed to an English word length.
- **Colour meanings:** post-office red is familiar and official in India, and red also means
  danger everywhere, so red is an ink for marks and stamps, never a ground and never the only
  signal. Green means go and money. No flag colour combinations. Risk tiers are told by
  position and label first, colour second, so colour-blind readers lose nothing.
- **Reading:** left to right in all four languages. Devanagari needs more line-height
  (about 1.6 to 1.7) and headroom above headlines.

## Feel in motion

Mechanical and liquid, never elastic.

- **The drain is the only linear motion** on the site, because it is a clock.
- **Marks land heavy:** a punch or stamp goes down in about 70 ms, holds 40 ms, releases in
  about 160 ms, with a 1 to 2 px settle and no bounce.
- **Paper slides with friction:** about 420 ms, strong ease-out, no overshoot. Tears follow
  the pointer, then let go.
- **Water is slow:** a floating bowl bobs on a loop of about 3 s with 2 to 3 px of travel;
  sinking takes about 900 ms easing in; lifting out takes about 320 ms easing out with one
  small settle.
- **Tempo:** slow ambient, short decisive actions. Nothing moves while it is being read.
  Nothing bounces more than once. Nothing loops faster than a calm breath.

## Material and texture

Tinted ticket paper as flat colour; one or two printing inks per surface; rule lines and grids
that are part of the structure; numerals in a grid; a serial number in its own face; stamp ink
with uneven coverage drawn as shape, not as a grunge texture; real holes that show what is
underneath; copper and water for the clock. No drop-shadow cards, no glass, no gradients as
decoration.

## Taste anchors

Objects, from the subject's own world:

- **The conductor's punched bus ticket.** One ink, a grid of numerals in the local script, a
  big fare block, and a single hole that records the decision. Right because the whole record
  fits in the hand and the mark cannot be edited afterwards.
- **The lottery ticket** of Mexico and the pools slip of Brazil. Two flat inks, a huge serial
  number also spelled out in words, an "expires" line. Right for how it makes a number and a
  deadline the heroes.
- **The savings passbook page.** Date, entry, balance in ruled columns, with a boxed stamp
  struck across them. Right for how calm a money record can be.
- **The circular date stamp.** A place, a time, a ring. Right for how it turns a moment into a
  mark that proves it happened.
- **The sinking-bowl clock.** Time as a thing you can see filling, and stop by hand.
- **The counter speaker that announces each payment.** Right for the voice: short, flat, in
  your language, impossible to misread.

Digital behaviour that already feels right, described without names: a block explorer page
where every value can be copied and checked; an instant-payment app whose success screen is one
big numeral and one tick; brand-guideline sites where every page has a small thing to play
with; a voice-product site on warm off-white paper that lets the words do the work.

## Lines not to cross

- No countdown that pulses, flashes, shakes or ticks aloud by default.
- No red or black full-bleed "danger" sections. A flagged recipient is shown with evidence, in
  the same calm layout as everything else.
- No scammer caricatures and no victim caricatures.
- No fake ageing: coffee stains, torn photo edges, crumpled paper textures, distressed filters.
- No imitation of a real operator's ticket, a state emblem, a bank logo, a currency note or a
  postal mark. Our tickets and stamps are originals that follow the grammar.
- No religious or sacred imagery as decoration. The bowl clock is drawn as an instrument.
- No script as ornament: Devanagari, Spanish and Portuguese appear only as real, correct text.
- No chain-brand gradient as a ground. Partner marks appear only in the partner sections.
- The word "safe" is never promised.

## Comfort

Phones in daylight, sometimes direct sun, on mid-range Android; judges at a desk, often late.
Readers from about 18 to 60, including parents receiving remittances. So: a **light ground**
(tinted paper, not white), dark ink at high contrast, body text at 16 px or more, 44 px touch
targets, numerals large. Dark ground is not chosen anywhere on the public site; the reasons
that would justify it (night-only use, a screen people stare at for hours) do not apply.

## What was assumed

Derived from the project plan, the product's own copy rules and research into the subject's
world; no user interviews. Assumed: no existing brand; judges read English; phone-first for
the second audience; sound off by default.
